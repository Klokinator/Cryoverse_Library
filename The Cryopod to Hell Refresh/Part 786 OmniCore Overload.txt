Part 786: OmniCore Overload

_January 29th, 2021. Aevum._

After giving Brian the good news, Jason then went on to notify Jerome and Farrah about Nadia's suggestion. Naturally, Jason was not going to shove the OmniCore down their throats. If they wanted to use a different MindCore, he'd be fine with that too.

Farrah went along with Nadia's decision. She respected Nadia, and she didn't have strong opinions one way or the other, plus Nadia's arguments successfully got her excited for the future.

Jerome was more puzzled than anything. First he was going to get a MindCore, but then he wasn't, and now he was again? He was certainly confused, but when he heard about the fact it would make him a lot smarter, he pounced on the opportunity.

Jerome hadn't had many good opportunities come his way, growing up. It wasn't that he was some rebellion thug who hated the system, but more that the simple opportunity to finish school had never been afforded to him. Thus, he was happy to get another leg up and potentially improve the power of his brain to match his body.

After Jason left, Jerome massaged his chin thoughtfully. He remembered the secret Nadia had told him... the truth about his powers, or what she thought they really were.

The fact Nadia suggested this MindCore was definitely related. Jerome knew she had a good reason for suggesting it, possibly an ulterior one. But he wasn't going to look a gift horse in the mouth. He wanted to become the best man possible, if not for himself, at least for his girl.

...

Time passed. Like this, four days came and went.

Jason and Nadia prepped for the next round of surgeries. This was the first time Jason had done three in a row. He'd done two back-to-back for Sasha and Helen, and he'd been so exhausted afterward that he felt like he'd half-died. Thus, three was definitely beyond his current capacity. He decided to spread the surgeries out, giving himself eight hours of rest between each one, with two hours of post-surgery time before the rest to make sure each person was acclimated, for a total of ten hours' downtime between surgeries. He didn't want to have even Sasha's minor side-effects popping back up if at all possible.

Soon, Brian found himself in the surgery chair. He was a little nervous, and anxious as hell, but he was also visibly excited. After seeing for himself all the benefits of Jason's MindCores, he couldn't wait to personally experience what they were really like!

"You've gotta calm down," Jason said, seeing Brian literally shaking from nerves. "You're usually way chiller than this."

"You don't understand," Brian said with a fierce grin. "No longer will I only be a rich daddy's boy with extremely good looks and kickass magical powers. I shall also become extremely smart too! Women will love me, and men will fear me. This is the day my rise becomes meteoric."

"You're already a pretty smart guy, despite lacking in humility," Jason said with a snort. "Me and Nadia have a wager about whether the OmniRecord has more effect on an already-smart person versus a lower-intellect one. It'll be interesting to see the results."

"Yes, yes, interesting indeed!" Brian chirped. He swept back his hair and took a long, deep breath to try and calm down. "So, um, when do we start?"

Jason milled around and muttered to Nadia under his breath. The two of them kept their voices low and confirmed a few things, just quiet enough that Brian couldn't hear them.

"...Okay. We are ready," Nadia said, activating her PepScan and walking over to surveil the situation from the side. "Ready when you are, Jason."

Jason nodded. Without further ado, he began the surgery.

Time passed. Brian shivered in pain and gritted his teeth periodically, digging his nails into the surgery chair's arms. His body shifted around as Jason rapidly cast tens upon tens of Words of Power, forming the nexus of Brian's future brainpower within his Mind Realm.

Before long, that one hour became two. Brian was in so much pain he really thought he would die. Even Jason started to get worried. It turned out Brian already had the latent form of a Mind Realm in his head, one which had to be destroyed and rebuilt. That slowed down the process noticeably compared to Sasha and Helen's operations.

But soon, the agony dimmed, and Brian's body relaxed. He twitched every so often, his skin soaked with sweat. His clothes were a mess, and his hair stuck to his scalp.

"Uh... uh..." Brian moaned. "D-done? Is it...?"

"Yeah," Jason said, visibly fatigued. He leaned heavily on the back of the surgery chair, taking long breaths while resting his eyes. "Christ. That was like cutting apart a boulder with a plastic knife. I don't know what your fucking head is made of, Brian, but you're lucky I've had so much practice. This would have gone way worse if you were my second MindCore surgery ever."

Five minutes passed. The phantom pain continued to tingle around Brian's head. It took a while for it to fade away. He felt as if he had the migraine of a lifetime, and even the already-dimmed overhead lights were burning his eyes whenever he opened them just a crack.

At the same time, Jason slumped back in a chair, unable to resist as Nadia dabbed at his forehead with a cold washcloth. He didn't even have the energy to push her away like usual. She guessed it probably took him three or four times the effort to convert Brian's Mind Realm into a MindCore compared to other people's, except for maybe Jason himself.

Eventually, Jason pulled himself back together. He recovered, and so did Brian. The light came back into Brian's eyes, and he sat up in the chair, blinking slowly as his brain fog dissipated.

"I don't... don't feel any smarter," Brian said, his words slightly wobbly. "Just have a headache."

"That's because we decided to put all MindCores into an inactive state following surgeries," Nadia explained. "Unfortunately, the headache is a side effect we can't bypass. When MindCores are fully disabled, it's as if they are drawing energy from your soul to keep themselves inactive. They need to be at least slightly activated at all times, or you'll get a headache."

"Well, can you turn it on then?" Brian grumbled. "Show me the magic."

Nadia looked at Jason. He was still laying back, defeated, his eyes closed. He managed a light nod. This next part was her domain, her main contribution to future surgeries.

As such, Nadia tapped into her PepScan. She looked inside the dormant MindCore within Brian's head. She couldn't see much further than the exterior layer, but that was enough for her purposes.

"I can't turn on your MindCore, but you can, Brian," Nadia explained. "Follow my instructions closely. First, close your eyes. To activate your MindCore, you must first be able to sense its presence within your mind. After that, you need to turn your attention over to this area here..."

Nadia started guiding Brian through the new startup procedures. These hadn't existed for earlier users, but after all the mishaps Jason had encountered, including accidentally locking Nadia within a simulation for seventy years, he wasn't taking chances anymore.

The new wake-up procedures were extremely complex. They weren't only designed to activate the MindCores, but also to run diagnostics and ensure everything was running perfectly. It was essentially one final chance for Jason to tweak the systems before truly booting a MindCore into motion.

Fifteen minutes passed. Brian passed all the initial checks. Nadia let out a quiet breath she didn't even realize she'd been holding.

"Alright. Five percent power. Initiate," Nadia ordered.

Brian's heart skipped a beat.

This was the moment of truth.

He activated the MindCore, increased its power to 5%, and all at once, everything changed.

His headache vanished. The words coming from Nadia's mouth seemed to slow down. A stream of thoughts poured into his head, arriving from all different directions.

Brian's eyes flicked to the left and the right.

Was it just his imagination? The world suddenly seemed more colorful than before. The 'richness' of the white walls now seemed more brilliant than before. The dirty smudges on the floor, caused by various footwear skidding across the tile, now seemed to pop out in an unsightly manner.

Brian looked at Nadia. Her mouth was half-open. It was starting to close as she finished a word. He watched as her lips pressed together, then opened again. He noted the position of her tongue against her upper molars. It seemed she was enunciating the letter 'T'.

Brian's eyes flicked over to Jason. He noticed that Jason's skin seemed artificially 'tighter' than it probably should. The result of Jason's magic used to keep his body young? Had he used his magic to keep himself looking eighteen?

Of course he had. Brian recalled that Jason had mentioned as much in passing. But it was only now that Brian actually noticed the physical details. He also noticed Jason's slightly puffy eyes. He'd really pushed himself during the surgery.

"-riiiiian?" Nadia said, slowly waving her hand.

Brian turned his attention back to her. "Yes?"

"It looks like the initiation was successful?" Nadia repeated.

Brian blinked. "Yes. It was a success."

Nadia tapped the side of her PepScan. She nodded slowly. "So it is. How are you feeling?"

Brian didn't answer immediately. He remained silent for a while. It felt like fifteen minutes. It might have only been fifteen microseconds.

"I am well," Brian said. "The headache is gone. My mind is... clear. Very clear. Much more than it has ever been."

Nadia looked a little worried. "Are you sure?"

Brian stared off into the distance. He seemed to mentally drift away, not quite listening to her.

"Brian? Brian, hello?" Nadia asked.

The young man blinked his eyes a few times. "He lied to me. Hah. I only just now realized it."

"Who? Jason?" Nadia asked, frowning.

"No. My father. When I was seven years old," Brian said, still staring at the wall. "He told me our dog ran away. But thinking back, his pupils were slightly dilated, and my mother's eyes looked ever so slightly moist. I hadn't noticed at the time, but it seems obvious in retrospect that she was crying. The dog must have died. But how? How did he die? Perhaps struck by a car? Simply passing of old age? Twelve years is about the average for a house-dog, even if ours had a luxurious life."

Brian lowered his eyes and tapped his lip thoughtfully. "I'll have to look into the matter. There are definitely clues I can investi- ah."

"Ah?" Nadia repeated.

"Yes. Sorry. I just remembered when my Aunt Mary passed away. I forgot about her. She used to give me sugar cookies when my mother wasn't looking. I thought we were being sneaky together. But now I realize my mother was in on it the whole time. She must have been trying to foster a relationship between her sister and me. Understandable. I was a bit mischievous, and wouldn't have eaten the cookies if I thought they were intended for my consumption. It was more fun to 'steal' or 'sneak' the cookies. But it was all a ruse. My mother and aunt lied to me with the best of intentions. How adorable."

Brian went quiet for a moment.

"...Can't believe I forgot about my Aunt Mary. I haven't thought about her in seven years, six months, and five days. She was so good to me, but I simply forgot about her. Wasn't that just awful of me?"

Nadia was quiet for a moment. She took a step toward Brian and rested her hand on his shoulder. "It wasn't your fault, Brian. This MindCore is designed to enhance memories and thinking potential. As people age, they tend to slowly forget even important things from earlier in their lives."

Brian wasn't overly emotional. He simply nodded. "You're right. I was just musing about it. Right, oh, ahaha. So that was the answer. I can't believe I cracked under pressure and forgot like that."

He looked up at Nadia and smiled. "It was B, not C. Such a silly mistake."

"B, not C??" Nadia asked.

"Yes, on my chemistry homework, a couple years ago. I kept sneaking peeks at this cute girl in the class, Ali. Then I got to question 15 and I couldn't stop thinking about her, so I randomly picked C, but the answer was actually B. I even knew the answer too, I just kept imagining the two of us having sex on a beach, and the daydream was so erotic I-"

"Brian. _Please,_" Nadia growled, sucking in a breath. "Please, control yourself. You're babbling about things I don't want to hear."

"Ahh, sorry. You're right. I'm talking too much," Brian said, quickly turning away from her. "Not that sex on the beach makes sense anyway, what with all the sand getting into places it shouldn't."

"Brian!" Nadia snapped.

"Oh, dear. I'm embarrassing myself," Brian said, lightly facepalming. "It's just... all of a sudden, countless memories hitting me at once. I remember every second of my life with crystal clarity, and I have to parse those memories, all the awkward moments, all my successes and failures. It's a bit overwhelming."

Nadia pursed her lips. Her memory had improved immeasurably since she obtained the RealitySim, but not to the level Brian's clearly had. She realized for the second or third time just how different each MindCore user experienced the world around them. She didn't envy Brian, but she didn't _not_ envy him, either.

"You'll... you'll get over it," Nadia said, though she wasn't entirely sure if that was true or not.

"Hopefully," Brian said softly. "Because otherwise- ah _dammit_, it was Steve who left that giant turd in my toilet six years ago. I knew it was him! I just didn't notice him hunching over slightly during the party, like he'd just dropped a monster poo! How could I have been so blind?!"

Nadia's eye twitched. "I'm... really sorry you have to remember all that, Brian."

Time passed. Brian kept cursing when he remembered some memories, and smiling or smirking when he remembered others. By the time Jason had somewhat recovered, Brian was still sorting through his childhood, so they sent him home to recuperate and sit with his thoughts.

"Well. Success. No side effects," Nadia said. "Other than maybe a loosening of the filter on his lips. It turns out Brian has had _quite_ the active sex life, and has gone to many more parties than I expected."

"He's a rich kid," Jason said with a shrug. "That's just his world, I guess. I'll take a long eight-hour sleep, then move on to... who do you think? Jerome, or Farrah?"

"Farrah," Nadia answered. "Jerome already has a new toy to play with. Ladies first, he can practice with the H-Boy while we operate on Farrah."

"Yeah, that's fair," Jason said. "I'm out for eight hours. Back later."

He vanished after uttering a Word of Power, leaving Nadia behind to clean up the sweat-soaked chair and prep Farrah for the next surgery.

...

Eight hours later, Jason rolled out of bed and washed his face. He cracked his neck and used Wordsmithing to make himself more presentable. He hopped over to Nadia's room and knocked on her door, then she joined him as they teleported over to the surgery bay.

Thirty minutes later, Jason started working on Farrah's MindCore. Unlike Brian, she was a lot easier to work with, and the surgery only took a little over an hour. Jason was tired afterward, and Farrah had a headache like Brian, but turning on her MindCore solved that problem, and she went through her own set of realizations and discoveries.

"A-amazing!" Farrah gasped. "I can hear colors! How is this possible...?"

Nadia cocked her head. "You said you can 'hear' colors?"

"Oh yes, it's beautiful..." Farrah whispered. "Nadia, your voice is so beautiful, but I can hear the sounds of love and sorrow in every tone. And my voice, there's a sense of melancholy, while Jason's has a tone of loss, and sadness... why is Jason so sad?"

Jason was slumped back in his chair again. His eyes were closed, but he chuckled nonetheless. "Probably because I've lost a lot. Not a big mystery there."

"Oh..." Farrah said softly, her gaze downturned. "I didn't realize... it must be really hard for you, Jason. But you're still trying to make the world better for people like me..."

"Don't worry about it, it's just what I do," Jason joked. His tone was dry, and it only made Farrah a little sadder.

She tried to ignore the 'musical colors' she was hearing, but it turned out this new quirk of her brain had been permanently enabled, and as long as her MindCore was active, she'd have to get used to it.

"I can remember so many things now," Farrah said, smiling at first, but then slowly fading away. "So many things... I remember every time my mother and father hit me. I remember boys teasing me when I was younger. I remember people calling me names. I... do not like many of these memories."

Nadia wrapped an arm around Farrah's shoulder. "I'm sorry, Farrah. There are a lot of memories people have that are unpleasant, or even outright terrible. You know, you can use your MindCore to delete the memories now. You don't have to live with them."

Farrah shook her head. "The memories make me who I am. Even the terrible ones. If I forget them, won't I forget who I am as a person? I don't want to think about them, but all that means is I'm hiding from the truth. I... I need to spend time with Jerome. He always makes me feel better."

"Okay," Nadia said, smiling as she lightly rubbed Farrah's back. "That's a good idea. Go stay with Jerome a while. We'll operate on him next, but it'll be in eight or so hours."

"Yes. I will, then. Thank you, Nadia," Farrah said, forcing a smile.

She got up and left. After she was gone, the surgery bay was silent for a while.

"Everyone's carrying baggage," Jason mumbled. "Everyone. Some people, many people, just don't realize it. You still think this OmniCore is a good idea?"

"It is," Nadia said, her eyes flickering with uncertainty. "It's not perfect, but nothing ever is. Even despite our best efforts, we can minimize some forms of negative MindCore side effects, but others will always pop up. I don't know if reliving past trauma is a better or worse outcome than being stuck in a mind-prison for seventy years. At least it's... real? But the upsides of the OmniCore will drastically outweigh the downsides, given time."

"I hope you're right. I'm just too tired to argue," Jason said, pressing his hand on the washcloth covering his forehead and eyes. "Fuuuuck. I'm... gonna go get some sleep again. This shit is exhausting."

Jason muttered a Word of Power, then he vanished from the surgery bay.

...

After leaving to head home, Farrah's movements were slow. She started to head back to the dorms, but she happened to notice Brian sitting in the local park, just a few blocks away from the surgery bay. Brian was sitting at a park bench, fully leaned forward, his palms flat on the table with his chin on top. His head was flopped to the side. He seemed to be lying there, unmoving, thinking about something.

"...Brian?" Farrah asked, as she walked a little closer.

Brian didn't move. His eyes turned slightly to look at her. "Oh, Farrah. Is your surgery over?"

Farrah nodded. She walked a little closer. "Are you okay?"

"I don't know," Brian said. "I think I am. I'm just not sure, though. There's a lot of conflicting thoughts bouncing around in here."

Farrah lowered her eyes. "Yes. Same. I... I keep remembering all sorts of good and bad things from my past... but it feels like the bad really outnumbers the good."

"It's the opposite for me," Brian said. "There's definitely far more good than bad... but the bad really sticks out. It keeps replaying in my head. I keep telling myself to look at the bright side of life, to see the silver lining, but I just can't stop thinking about every bad thing that's ever happened to me."

Farrah sat across from Brian at the table. She leaned heavily on her elbows, her posture sagging.

"I feel... I feel as if I'm continually re-traumatizing myself," Farrah said softly. "Sometimes, people say mean things to hurt your feelings. In the past, I'd try to ignore them. Eventually, I would forget them. But now, I remember as if it happened five seconds ago. And I remember all those mean things. My mama always told me I acted too much like a boy, and it made me ugly. So I tried to act more like a girl. But I didn't act girly enough. Or I acted too girly. She'd tell me I was overcompensating."

"Papa was the same," Farrah continued. "He told me I couldn't get a husband if I didn't act right. He made it seem as if the only way I could be a good girl was to get a husband who was rich and successful. I wanted to paint and write books, but he said those were traits of smarty-pants girls, and not something any daughter of his would do."

Brian lifted his head. He sat up a little straighter. "Really? Your own parents treated you like that? That's terrible!"

"Yes," Farrah said. "I only felt better when I went to school and spent time with my friends, but it was an all-girls school. I wasn't allowed to talk to boys. At the same time, I didn't fit in well with the other girls. There were all these rules about clothing, too. I hated them. But I had no choice. I had to follow the rules, or the teachers would punish me."

Brian chewed his lip. "Have you told Jerome any of this?"

"Yes... a little bit, here and there. But the memories always felt so distant and hazy, I didn't go into detail. Now it's all I can think about," Farrah said gloomily. "I feel awful. I wish I hadn't chosen this MindCore. I am scared it will hurt Jerome too. I don't want him to pick it..."

Brian and Farrah were both quiet for a time. Brian lowered his eyes, falling deep into thought.

"Hmm..." Brian grunted. "I don't know, Farrah. Right now, the memories are overpowering. We're fixated on them because they feel fresh. But instead of using them as a means of torturing ourselves, why not weaponize them for good?"

"Huh? How?" Farrah asked.

"The way I understand it, our brains are much more powerful than before," Brian explained. "Why not use your new smarts to think of ways of changing society? Turn the bad from your past into a weapon to fix the future. You're not the only girl suffering out there, right? But now you have the means to change it. You can... you can... I don't know. Lobby for political change? Use your new brainpower to change the world! At least your home country?"

"I'm no good at any of that stuff..." Farrah mumbled, dejected.

"Maybe you _weren't_, but that was before the MindCore," Brian said solemnly. "I bet if you really apply yourself, you can accomplish big things."

"What about you?" Farrah asked. "You have a MindCore, too, now. Are you going to do big things?"

Brian didn't immediately answer. He leaned back in his chair and looked up at the pitch-black sky. Aevum had no atmosphere, so its sky always looked like nighttime.

"Me... I'm not sure. I'm still working through some of my own stuff. Not on your level or anything, but still stuff I need to think about. I've always lacked drive. I just go with the flow."

Farrah nodded. "There's no reason to rush. I'm gonna go talk to Jerome. Maybe he can help me sort my thoughts."

"I guarantee he will," Brian said, smiling warmly at her. "See you later."

"Yeah. See you," Farrah said, smiling shyly.

She got up and darted away, leaving Brian to ruminate on his past.

After Farrah made it back to the dorms, she started to knock on the door, then remembered she lived there and didn't have to knock. So she stepped inside, but when she entered, Jerome wasn't there.

"Huh?" Farrah muttered. "Jerome?"

Suddenly, she felt a presence behind her. Farrah turned around to see Jerome standing there, hands on his hips. "There you are, baby. Man, I've been lookin' for you. I left for like five minutes to grab a snack, and when I got back, they told me the surgery was all done and you'd left!"

He quickly touched her cheek. "Baby, you good? You feelin' okay after they was diggin' around in ya head'n shit?"

Farrah nodded shyly. "I, um... yes. I'm fine... sort of. I don't know. All at once, my memories are so vivid... and I can't stop thinking about a lot of things..."

Jerome entered the room and closed the door. He and Farrah walked over and sat on the bed. Before long, she started pouring her heart out to him, then she cried, and he listened patiently, consoling her, hugging her, helping her work through her emotions.

"That's awful, baby. Damn. Can't believe all that shit happened to you. You ain't told me most of this before," Jerome grumbled. "I oughta smack the faces off some of these motherfuckers."

"I wasn't... wasn't thinking about it until the MindCore," Farrah said dejectedly. "Now it's all I can think about."

Jerome was quiet for a moment. "So you think I shouldn't get one?"

"It's scary. All the repressed memories, coming back up. Every failure. Every time people made fun of you, or mocked you, or insulted you. It's terrible," Farrah mumbled.

"Sounds like a form of continual pressure for my brain," Jerome said, his tone thoughtful. "Well, if you got one, I'm definitely getting one. Maybe it'll suck shit out my ass, but I ain't lettin' my girl go at it alone! I told you, baby, I'm always gonna be here for you and your struggles, aight? We in this together."

"Oh, Jerome..." Farrah smiled shyly, looking up at her boyfriend with loving eyes. "You... you always know how to make me feel better."

"Damn right, girl. I know how to make you feel _good_ too. Now, let's make some new memories to push out them shitty ones."

Jerome leaned in and passionately kissed Farrah. She kissed him back. After that, he laid her down on the bed.
