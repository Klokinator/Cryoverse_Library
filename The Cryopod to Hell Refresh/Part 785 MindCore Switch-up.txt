Part 785: MindCore Switch-up

_January 29th, 2021. Aevum._

Several hours later, Jason took some time to chat with Nadia privately. They stood inside the Spynet, the light of monitors flickering around them as Jason's secret network of spy drones kept an eye on the galaxy at large. The two of them reflected on what they had learned from the newest MindCore users.

"No negative side effects at all. Not a headache, no personality changes, and Helen is even operating her MindCore at 100%. This goes beyond my expectations," Jason said.

"Sasha had several side effects. There must be something different, or special, about Helen," Nadia suggested. "She's a Lazarite. She's much older than Sasha. Maybe her spirituality is more developed? Maybe her brain is more resilient?"

"I thought older people would have more trouble assimilating a MindCore. That's what the projections showed," Jason argued.

"It could still hold true. Helen is a deeply cerebral person," Nadia retorted. "Maybe other geriatrics will have decaying brains as a result of their lifespan drawing to its end. But Helen is an immortal angel, so her body is a lot more resilient than those of ordinary humans."

"This suggests that strengthening the spirit, the mind, and maybe even the body could be beneficial steps to take before implanting a MindCore," Jason muttered. "But even so, Sasha's side effects weren't that bad. Like me, she will eventually adapt to her MindCore more and more, and become fully capable of using it at greater power levels. If that's the biggest trade-off, then I'd say the side effects of MindCores are essentially solved."

Nadia went quiet for a moment. Jason guessed she was conversing with Echo internally.

"That seems about right," Nadia finally concluded. "I think we're a go for the final remaining MindCore users. We just need to keep an eye on Sasha and Helen for a few days before the next surgery."

Jason pulled up his itinerary. "So. We have Brian, Farrah, and Marco next. Jerome won't be getting one, at least for now. Did everyone finalize their choices yet?"

Jason had been quite busy with a lot of things, so he left this decision to Nadia, and also Echo. Echo would probably make a perfectly sensible decision, and Jason could always veto it if the decision felt wrong. He had the final say.

Nadia crinkled her nose. "Marco is obvious. We're still going with the HellProphet for him. Since his ability involves a form of time manipulation, prediction heuristics feel strong. As for Farrah and Brian, they were a lot harder to pin down."

She continued. "Let's start with Brian. He has flame powers. He's not much of a creative type, so the value of a GenesisFrame like yours is low. Being creative with his powers could be useful, but I am skeptical. Prediction matrices don't suit him either, so we can rule out the HellProphet. That leaves the RealitySim, the QuantumReflex, and the OmniRecord."

Jason nodded. "Simulations would be a great all-purpose way for him to improve his abilities holistically. Improving his raw combat reflexes is obviously going to go well, but a simple brain boost through the OmniRecord feels mediocre. We can cross that one off the list."

Nadia looked at Jason carefully. "So far, nobody has chosen the OmniRecord, and you've seemed ready to discard it on multiple occasions. Are you sure that's a wise decision?"

Jason shrugged. "Without Solomon's practically infinite repository of memories and knowledge, or his powers to fill up such a repository, the OmniRecord just feels like a bootleg QuantumReflex, but worse. It improves thinking speed, memory, and it can have a good effect on broad intelligence, but I don't see value in it otherwise."

"I completely disagree," Nadia said, making a karate-chopping motion toward Jason. "You have a strong bias against it because you focus too much on the 'innate knowledge' part of Solomon's Crown. You think that without that database, the OmniRecord is unimpressive. But from my point of view, the OmniRecord is like a combination of the RealitySim, the QuantumReflex, and the HellProphet, all in one."

Jason snorted. "You can't be serious. I've _gotta_ hear this argument."

"Okay. Let's do that then," Nadia said, crossing her arms, puffing out her lower lip, tilting her head slightly to the side, and nodding nonchalantly. "First off, a general brain boost is not to be underestimated. Most humans use less than 10% of their brain's capacity. Even the smartest ones only use small parts of it at once. The real benefit of the OmniRecord is that it lets you use the full power of your brain, enhances your thinking speeds and reaction speeds dramatically, and makes you more cognitively powerful. Do not underestimate these benefits."

She paused before adding, "You think the RealitySim is a good brain booster too, but actually it doesn't really improve a person's intelligence. It augments their thinking speed by allowing them to set up time-accelerated simulations within their head, then instantly download any new knowledge right into their psyche. But the truth is, there's a lot of lag involved. If someone isn't a fast thinker, they will struggle to make use of it. I'm special because my ability enhances my brain dramatically. Catherine is special because she's five hundred years old and a very intelligent woman who is much more educated than many of Earth's current genius humans."

Jason remained silent, allowing Nadia to continue with her argument.

And so, she did. "The value of the QuantumReflex is giving all sorts of animalistic abilities, improving reaction times, and turning an ordinary person into a predator, while enhancing existing combat-type humans into elite slaughtering juggernauts. This is absolutely useful, but it doesn't come with any special benefits to general thinking speed, to intelligence improvements, or to other broad brain-boosting augments."

"Finally, we have the HellProphet. Without Marco to give us data, I can best say that it doesn't really augment general thinking speed, but it drastically improves environmental awareness, pattern recognition, and the ability to parse links between factoids and reality. In theory, this allows one to predict future events based on the world around them. Marco could become a very profitable stock trader with the abilities he's about to get."

"I see," Jason said. "So you're saying that even without Solomon's repository of knowledge, the OmniRecord is like a combination of all three of those?"

"That's exactly right," Nadia answered. "First off, general thinking speed, in all situations, is enhanced. One's perception of reality can instantly slow to a crawl, giving them ample time to detect and react to incoming threats. If one is giving a speech, they will have plenty of 'mental time' to think about their words before speaking, boosting charisma. Then we can consider something like predictive threat analysis. Being able to look around and observe one's environment at high speeds naturally lends itself to detecting hidden threats, detail-oriented thinking, and other such things."

"But it gets _better_," Nadia added, leaning forward and putting her hands on her hips. She smiled at Jason. "You think the OmniRecord is inferior to Solomon's Crown because it lacks a vast, broad base of knowledge. But, put another way, can't the user acquire knowledge more directly suited to them with great ease?"

Jason raised an eyebrow. "I don't follow. How would having a narrow band of information be in any way comparable to having all of Solomon's knowledge?"

"Let me put it this way," Nadia said. "Right now, all MindCore users can share information, images, and mental videos by touching their fingers together and exchanging those thoughts. This is way more powerful in the mind of OmniRecord users, because they have practically infinite internal storage space. You are greatly held back by your internal storage limitations. There is only so much information you can stuff into your MindCore before you have to delete it or shove it into an external storage device."

Jason lowered his eyes. He knew Nadia was right. He'd created a massive library of all human knowledge, but he hadn't actually been able to assimilate the vast majority of it. Instead, whenever he had a new project, he would delete information from his GenesisFrame, download more relevant information, and use that to improve his current working item. If he didn't do this, his GenesisFrame would slow down dramatically and it would end up being no better than his normal brain.

Nadia continued. "Anyone who installs an OmniRecord _can_ use that library you've assembled, download it into their brains, and become instantly smarter. Not only that, but OmniRecord users can download the thoughts and experiences of other people, without limit, but only if other people also have MindCores."

At that moment, Jason's heart jumped. His eyes widened and he took a step back in shock. "Wait, you're talking about... a botnet! But for humans!"

"That's not the term I'd use, but yes," Nadia affirmed. "I can run a simulation, create the data, then pass it on to someone else with an OmniRecord. They can store it forever and learn from its results, instantly assimilating it into their knowledge. This means I can create training sims for specific types of combat and pass them on to other MindCore users. Of course, I could already do that, but the result is far less impressive with any other MindCore compared to the OmniRecord. I can create blueprints for GenesisFrame users, and I can simulate different animal biologies and instincts to improve QuantumReflexers. I can generate data to improve future prediction capabilities of a HellPhophet user."

"But all those things... are fully compatible with an OmniRecord user!" Jason exclaimed. "My god. You're right. I've been totally overlooking the value, all this time. It's not 'just' an all-purpose cerebral enhancement, and it's not 'useless' without Solomon's Knowledge. We can use the OmniRecord to create specialized combat personnel, hackers, all sorts of other things. We just give them the data for specialized domains and let them learn rapidly."

"Exactly," Nadia said. "I sort of think Helen would have been better off with an OmniRecord, but it's too late to change that now, and her true purpose is to create blueprints for Catherine anyway. It's a different use-case scenario."

"So your final conclusion is..." Jason said, after a long pause. "We should give Brian an OmniRecord?"

Several seconds passed. Nadia made an odd popping noise with her lips. "Not quite!"

"Huh? What does that mean?" Jason asked.

"I think that Brian should get an OmniRecord, but so should Farrah... and so should Jerome!"

Jason was stunned. "All three of them? Are you sure...?"

"Yes. I'm confident in my belief that all three of them would make for excellent users of the OmniRecord," Nadia said, revealing a satisfied smile.

"First, let's talk about Brian," she continued. "Brian is the son of a diplomat. He's good with words, he's charming, and he's not much of a fighter. I think if he leans into his verbal skills, he could become a talented politician, manipulator, and so on. His fire magic holds plenty of utility as well, and it could protect his life."

"You don't think he's vanguard material?" Jason asked. "Making him a politician feels kinda like a waste when he can shoot fireballs out of his hands!"

"Fireballs are nothing," Nadia snorted. "They look flashy, but they're slow and avoidable. Bullets and missiles are way more effective. I'm not saying Brian can't be a strong combatant, but compared to the Beelzebub of the future, he's way weaker in terms of 'fire' power."

Jason couldn't argue with that point. "Fine. That explains Brian, but what about Farrah? Hell, what about Jerome? I thought we weren't going to give him a MindCore just yet? Even if we were, wouldn't the QuantumReflex make more sense?"

"Not at all," Nadia said. "The QuantumReflex drastically enhances a person's 'instincts.' But Jerome already has solid battle instincts. Sure, he's never killed anyone, but he's done a lot of fighting in his youth. He doesn't need the QuantumReflex. What he needs is... a solid education and improvement of his mental capacities."

Jason squinted at her. "...I really hope you're not just saying that because he's black."

"What sort of woman do you take me to be?" Nadia scoffed. "I've looked into his records and spoken to him extensively over the last few months. Jerome didn't finish primary school. He never had that opportunity. He grew up poor in the foster system. But unlike you, he didn't get opportunities to go to high school, or college, or much of anything. Did you know he can barely read or write?"

"I... I didn't know that," Jason said, suddenly feeling guilty about his lack of talking with all the teenagers. "I could just Wordsmith his brain to be smarter, though. He doesn't need a MindCore for that."

"No, Jerome needs more than a band-aid fix," Nadia said, holding up her palm. "Trust me, I've run the simulations. Jerome with an OmniRecord is way more powerful and effective as a combatant than Jerome with any of the other four MindCores. It fixes a large gaping hole in his current capabilities. It also gives him access to an arsenal of abilities you'll come to appreciate later on."

Jason eventually acquiesced. It seemed Nadia had thought about this more than him, and he trusted her judgment. "Okay, fine. But Farrah, too? I guess the other MindCores don't help her much, but why the OmniRecord? Don't you think three people with the same MindCore is a bit... much?"

"If we count your father's micro-crown embedded inside his brain as a sort of OmniRecord, then technically there would be four," Nadia said. "For Farrah, I've thought about it, and what she really needs is a greater capacity, more thinking speed, and more... everything. The fact she suddenly gained the ability to change her body's appearance after accepting her gender identity indicates to me that she's actually being held back by deep self-esteem issues, like Marco. A lot more time spent on self-acceptance and reflection would do her good."

Jason tapped his lip thoughtfully. "That makes... sense. Nothing combats self-hatred like expanding your horizons. Since none of the other MindCores really seem like they would enhance her shapeshifting powers... alright then. A third OmniRecord it is."

"I know it changes your plans," Nadia said. "But this is better anyway. We'll upgrade Brian, Farrah, and Jerome first. Then we'll work on Marco last."

"I'm sure Marco will be thrilled to hear that," Jason said dryly. "But fine. In four days, we'll work on those three, then a week after that... Marco."

"Sounds good to me," Nadia said with a smile.

The two of them concluded their business. Jason updated his itinerary and left with a flicker of motion, departing the Spynet and leaving Nadia behind.

With a Word of Power, he immediately located Brian, but he raised an eyebrow. Brian was outside Aevum, and he wasn't alone.

Jason teleported into the very same racing arena he had built for Phoebe a week earlier. He was surprised to see her there, as well as Brian and Helen, to boot.

Phoebe was the first to notice Jason's appearance. She was standing in front of the Ferrari Jason had Wordsmithed, off to the side a little, its hood popped open. Brian was right in the center, leaning over and looking into the car's internals, his hands black with oil stains. Helen was on the other side, her back to Jason.

"Hello, Jason!" Phoebe said with a big smile. "What brings you here?"

Jason smiled back. "I came to talk to Brian... what's all this about?"

Helen turned around to look at Jason. "Oh, hello again, Jason. I was talking to Phoebe and she mentioned you'd taken her on a car ride. Brian happened to overhear us, so we all came over here to have a look at it."

"Seems like a bit more than a _look_," Jason said, gesturing to Brian's filthy arms. "Something wrong with the car?"

Brian pulled away. He looked a little embarrassed. "Not at all, Jason. It's just... I work on cars for a hobby, and I heard you'd conjured a Ferrari, so I pestered Phoebe to let me come tinker with it. Ah... I hope that's alright with you?"

Jason nodded. "It's fine, no problem at all. I can summon a whole fleet of them if you want. But why are you tinkering with it?"

Phoebe lowered her eyes. She looked a little uncomfortable. "Well... it's just... when you took me driving... I kept feeling as if the car was... rather slow. I wanted it to go faster. Brian said upgrading its speed was something he might be able to help with."

"Oh... I see," Jason said.

It was at this moment multiple realizations entered his mind.

_That's right. I originally took Phoebe driving when we met in the future... but she didn't have a MindCore. She was scared and exhilarated by the car's speed because she had a normal human mind. But maybe that didn't happen in this era because of the Sphinx's power? Maybe her mental perception is so high that a hundred miles an hour felt like ten? I might as well have been slowly ambling around a neighborhood. No wonder she seemed so bored!_

Another thought followed. _And she looks embarrassed now... maybe she didn't want to hurt my feelings? Damn! If I'd known we weren't going fast enough, I could have easily increased the speed by multiple factors! This silly woman..._

It turned out Phoebe was embarrassed because she'd seen all the effort Jason had gone to, and she didn't want to sound ungrateful, but she probably thought 100 MPH was as fast as the Ferrari went. Thus, wouldn't she be insulting his present if she implied it wasn't good enough? So she kept quiet and went to other people instead.

But now that Jason had abruptly appeared, she was caught in the act. She felt ashamed, like a kid getting caught with their hand in the cookie jar.

Jason scratched his head and smiled. "Ah... damn. Phoebe, it's totally fine if the Ferrari wasn't fast enough to give you a thrill. I can just use my magic to make it go way faster, you know."

"What? You can?" Phoebe asked, raising her head and looking at him in surprise. "That's really... possible?"

"Yeah. Absolutely," Jason said with a vigorous nod. "Shoot, you guys are going to all this trouble when I could have fixed the problem in ten seconds. I feel silly..."

Helen frowned. "Now hold on just a second, young man. You seem to be implying that what we're doing is a waste of time. But I happen to be enjoying putting all my new knowledge to work, Brian enjoys tinkering on cars, and Phoebe was excited to see her hopes coming to fruition. It's fine if you can solve this problem in mere seconds, but do not denigrate human time and effort just because you think it's less efficient."

Jason was taken aback. He'd never expected Helen to chide him verbally like this. It almost seemed like it came out of left field.

But he quickly thought about the matter. He'd just given Helen a new MindCore. She probably heard Phoebe's complaints about the car, downloaded a few hundred books on auto mechanics, and was going to fix it by herself when they encountered Brian. Brian offered to help, and so the three of them ended up here.

"Alright, uh, sorry," Jason apologized. "You're right. Just because my magic can solve any problem doesn't mean it should be the only solution. Do you... want my help this time?"

Brian looked at his filthy arms. He seemed hesitant. "Ahh... well, I mean, the goal is simply to speed up the car... realistically if I work on it for a few months, I might be able to get it up to 150 MPH, but... that probably won't be nearly fast enough to sate Miss Berthold's appetite."

Jason reflected on Brian's words. "If you want, I can just Wordsmith another Ferrari that goes way faster, and this one can be yours, Brian. Tinker on it, rip it apart, do what you want. I don't mind. Nothing wrong with a guy having a hobby."

Brian's smile brightened. "Really? If it's okay with you, I'd appreciate that. I may not look the type, but I do love tinkering with mechanical stuff. I wish I was better at it, but I'm fine with treating it as a hobby."

"Really? I didn't know you were into this," Jason said, his eyebrow raising slightly. "Like I said, I actually came here to talk to you, Brian. You have a minute?"

"Of course, of course," Brian said before looking down at his filthy arms. He grabbed a rag and rubbed it against the grease with great fervor, but it mainly just smeared the tar around and didn't do much to clean him.

"**Clean.**" Jason said, instantly returning Brian's arms to their normal level of cleanliness. "Come on, over here, just a minute or two."

Like this, Jason pulled Brian aside and ran over the new plans with him. Brian's eyes lit up when he heard the news.

"You previously told me the OmniRecord wasn't too useful, but now I'm extremely interested in it!" Brian said. "If your description is true, it could make me a better mechanic, a better orator, and more creative with my magic as well. I'll definitely jump at the opportunity. It suits me more than the other MindCores."

Jason patted Brian's shoulder. "Great! Glad you like the idea. I didn't want to make a decision on your behalf, but Nadia was convincing enough that I thought it was worth running this by you. Err, didn't she talk to you about this already?"

Brian shrugged. "Nope. Didn't say anything to me. I mean, we talk all the time, but she hadn't mentioned a MindCore switch-up. You said you're going to give the same one to Farrah and Jerome, though?"

"Yeah," Jason grunted. "Nadia's arguments were... pretty convincing. I'll leave you to the Ferrari. Gotta meet up with the other two."

"Thanks," Brian said. He started to turn away, but then he paused. "By the way, Jason... thank you. Again. Seriously."

"Huh?" Jason asked. "Come on, don't get all sappy on me now."

"No. Really, thank you," Brian said, softly smiling. There was a slight look of sadness in his eyes. "I've really enjoyed my time here at Aevum. I just... ever since I obtained my powers... my life felt like it wasn't going well. I accidentally burned down a warehouse and nearly killed innocent people. My father was angry at me... and he was scared of me. I was scared, too. I didn't know what I was becoming."

Brian's arms hung at his sides. He looked off into the distance. "The Illuminati found out about me. They offered to teach me to use my powers. My father forced me to join them. Immediately, my life became smaller in every way. I was monitored all the time. I lost my freedom. The Illuminati weren't bad people. They didn't hurt or mistreat me. They said everything was for 'my own protection,' but I felt like a rat in a cage."

Jason stared at Brian's side profile. "Was it really that bad? I had no idea."

"We weren't allowed to leave the facility without an escort. We had to have guards with us at all times. The hassle alone meant nobody ever left. Especially me, they were really overprotective because of my father's sensitive status. He's kind of a big deal. So they had to 'protect' and monitor me all the time. But ever since I came here, it feels like the shackles have come off. I even get to mess around with cars. A Ferrari, at that! I've made friends. I've become stronger. It's all such a big step-up from living at the Illuminati."

Brian lifted his head to smile at Jason more sincerely. "Jerome and Farrah are a little different, in that the Illuminati was better than what they had on the streets, but Aevum is an even bigger step up. I'm so glad to see them living their best life. Farrah smiles a lot more, lately. Jerome is always full of energy. You're making a difference in all our lives, even if you're mainly focused on fighting demons in the future."

"I... I see," Jason said quietly. "I hadn't really given it much thought. I'm glad you guys are having a good time. Ah... I'll try and make Aevum even better to live in, moving forward."

"That's why I like you, Jason," Brian said as he reached over and squeezed Jason's shoulder. "You don't even know what a good guy you are. It comes naturally to you. If you'd met the people I've met, you'd come to understand how rare that quality is in people with power."

Brian pulled his hand away. He turned to leave. "Well, if you'll excuse me, I've got a hot date with a hot rod. Thanks for letting me work on the Ferrari, Jason. I'll treat her well."

Jason nodded. "You're welcome. See you later, Brian. Enjoy yourself."

Like that, Brian departed. Jason sighed softly, then he flickered away too. After this, he had to talk to Farrah and Jerome, then move on to the rest of his itinerary.

He was always so busy these days; it was only after his talk with Brian that he realized other people were actually living rich, full lives.

Secretly, Jason envied Brian. Brian's powers might be lesser than Wordsmithing, but he didn't have the weight of the world on his shoulders. Jason missed being able to kick back, play video games, or just hang out with his family.

"Hmm. I should take Daisy out to eat some good food sometime," Jason muttered to himself. "A father-daughter date. I want to spend more time together with my little girl... while she's still my little girl."
