Part 784: Big Brain Time

_January 29th, 2021. Aevum._

Jason left Nadia and Jerome behind. He teleported back to the teenagers outside the medical facility, only to pause on arrival and look around in confusion.

"Marco? Brian? Where's Sasha?"

Jason fired the words out involuntarily due to surprise, only to scratch his head and add in, "Heck, where's Daisy and Farrah?"

Marco shrugged. "After you left with Jerome, well, Sasha started acting real weird. She told us she needed to go back to her room, and Daisy seemed a little worried for her, so she took Sasha and Farrah both back to Sasha's room."

"We're not certain what the problem was." Brian added in. He was lazily munching on a hot-dog, seemingly carefree without a worry in the world. "Daisy is Sasha's best friend. It's best to let the girls sort these things out."

"Yeah, maybe you're right... I'll go check on them anyway." Jason said, suddenly feeling spooked by the prospect of Sasha going mad or suffering a mental break like Catherine and Nadia.

Jason spoke a Word of Power, then disappeared from the spot.

"What do you think is going on?" Marco asked after Jason left.

"Don't know." Brian answered nonchalantly. "If the Archseer can't solve the problem, we sure can't either. I just hope it's not a big deal, or it might make me second guess whether I should install my MindCore."

Marco looked unperturbed. "Whether I get one or not, I doubt it'll make any difference. I just hope Sasha's okay."

...

Jason appeared outside the door to Sasha's dormitory. He could heal muffled voices and noises inside, but he had no idea what was going on.

"Hey, Sasha? You in there?" Jason asked while knocking the door. "Daisy? Farrah? Hello?"

A short few seconds passed. The door opened, and Farrah peeked out at him.

"Oh, uhm, Jason..." She said. She had reverted back to her female form for some reason. This was odd, as she usually preferred to look boyish lately.

"Is everything okay with Sasha?" Jason asked.

Farrah looked hesitant. "I don't... uhm... I don't know. She's acting a little-"

"WRONG! All WRONG!" Sasha shrieked, making Farrah jump. She looked like a cat startled by a loud noise.

"Is everyone decent in there?" Jason asked.

Farrah nodded, so he let himself in.

When the door fully opened and Jason strode inside, he paused at the entrance and slowly raised one eyebrow as high as it could go.

Sasha's room was a mess. It looked like a bomb had gone off. She had sloppily yanked all the clothes out of her dressers and drawers, thrown them on the bed, on the floor, and on top of random tables and chairs. Her dresser drawers were open in various states, and overall the room had become a kaleidoscope of colors.

"Oh, hey dad." Daisy said, walking over. The fact she hadn't even noticed him until now showed just how distracted she was.

Sasha was bouncing around, grabbing random clothing articles, throwing them on the bed, spreading them out, and then making faces that ranged from pure horror to the look of disgust a mother might give if she walked in on two children eating each other's boogers. She didn't look happy at all, and even seemed at her wit's end.

"Awful! Every single one! God! How could I be such a failure?? I was so happy with my wardrobe until today, now I feel as though I were wearing third-generation hand-me-downs! What an embarrassment! I'm going to have to redesign every piece from scratch! Even my favorite yellow sundress!!"

Jason scratched his head. At this point, he'd pretty much figured out what happened. Daisy and the other teenagers weren't around when he had installed his MindCore, so they weren't familiar with the GenesisFrame's intricacies. Even Daisy had no idea what exactly her father's MindCore did for him, beyond making him 'more creative'. Since she had a MindCore of her own, she vaguely assumed his was similar to hers.

But it wasn't. Daisy's MindCore was focused on combat. Jason's was aimed more at research, creativity, and deep thinking. He now felt a little bad, leaving Sasha to fend for herself for the last 40 minutes or so. It seemed the MindCore had totally overloaded the fashion-side of her brain.

"It's going to be all right, Sasha!" Jason shouted, startling Sasha out of her disarrayed thinking patterns. She turned to look at him as if he were a random stranger harassing her on the street.

"What are you shouting for?!" Sasha snapped. "Lower the volume, you loudmouth! Can't you see I'm busy overhauling my wardrobe??"

Jason crossed his arms and smiled. "Overwhelming, isn't it? All of a sudden, you've started noticing the world around you a lot more sharply. The way things are designed, the contours, coloring, the shape and intricate details. For you, you're probably paying a lot more attention to the materials of your clothes than you were before. Isn't that right?"

Sasha still seemed distracted. She kept trying to look at her clothes, but Jason's words nibbled at the back of her mind.

"Yes. It's very annoying!" Sasha spat. "All these flaws, and I was blissfully ignorant of them before. But I can't be ignorant anymore. These clothes are all so crude-looking, and I was completely wrong about the color patterns too!"

Jason nodded. He remembered when he installed his MindCore and he first realized his casually-chosen clothes were sloppy and hideous. Even after dialing his MindCore down, he still ultimately reworked his wardrobe. Now he tended to dress business-casual, with long-sleeved plaid shirts and slacks, as well as good dress shoes. Not a tie though. He wasn't that far gone yet.

For Sasha, her clothing-sense was extremely potent, while her architecture-sense was more muted. Jason mentally noted the discrepancy. As expected, the personality of a person was 'expanded upon' when they obtained a MindCore. He wondered how Helen was doing.

"Alright, I'm aware of the flaws." Jason said. "Follow my directions, and we'll dial the strength of your GenesisFrame down to a more tolerable limit."

After a few minutes, he managed to bring Sasha's hyperactive thinking process down to a tolerable level. She was still annoyed by all the flaws in her clothes, but the hyper-compulsive need to immediately fix them had faded away. Now they were only a bit of an eyesore she'd need to work on fixing slowly, over the next few days.

"Thanks." Sasha said to Jason. "I thought I was going mad. Every flaw I saw was bringing my mood down, and the more flaws I spotted the more I was able to spot after that!"

"You'll get used to it." Jason reassured her. "Come with me. We need to include Helen in the discussion."

Daisy and Farrah stayed behind. After dealing with Sasha's neurotic episode for 45 minutes, they were fine letting Jason take over. Jason teleported himself and Sasha over to Helen and Harold's house.

Jason didn't know what to expect. He hadn't dialed down Helen or Sasha's MindCores to a lower level. Given that Sasha had lost her mind, just as he had during his first activation, he imagined Helen was probably going apeshit on baking a cake or something. Was she nagging Harold half to death? Anything seemed possible.

The two arrived outside Harold's house and Jason rang the doorbell. It wasn't long before Helen and Harold both arrived, all smiles.

"Hello, dear." Helen said, her face as sweet as ever. "Need something?"

Jason blinked. Helen seemed completely normal. "Uh, not exactly. I came to talk to you about your MindCore. I brought Sasha along... she sort of almost had a mental breakdown because her MindCore was too potent. Um... have you experienced any... changes in behavior?"

Jason looked at Harold as he asked this. The old man was now in the body of a forty-something, so he had the air of an authoritative adult, but he still had some 'old geezer' mannerisms. He hemmed and hawed for a moment, then shrugged.

"Nope." Harold said. "Helen seems 'bout the same. Nothin' weird I've noticed, kid."

Jason raised an eyebrow. Had he failed to activate her MindCore at all? Maybe he accidentally left it off?

"Nothing at all?" Jason pressed.

This time, Helen tapped her lip and looked upward thoughtfully. "Nothing really comes to mind, dear. Oh, well, I have been feeling more calm and at-ease since the operation. I had the urge to sit down and read a book. Plucked 'Fahrenheit 451' off the shelf. What an interesting read! I was completely engrossed in it until you rang."

Jason looked confused. It wasn't a book he'd read, but more importantly, the fact she was just calmly reading a book... could this even be considered the MindCore's effect? Wasn't Helen always sitting around, reading books when she had nothing else to do? Even in Heaven, she was a heavy reader, according to Catherine.

"I should check and make sure your MindCore is active." Jason said. "Mind if I check?"

"By all means! You're the good doctor, after all!" Helen chuckled good-naturedly. "Do we need to go back to the operation room?"

"Not at all. Just give me a moment. **Inspect.**"

Jason uttered a Word of Power. He delved inside the outer layers of Helen's MindCore, close enough to give it a quick once-over.

_The hell?_ Jason internally wondered. _It's active, 100% power level. Why hasn't her personality changed at all? Don't tell me Helen's as dumb as a rock or something?_

Jason himself always kept his MindCore hovering around 75%. Any higher and it felt rather irritating. He'd dialed it up from 25% over time, and he expected Sasha would do the same as the days and weeks passed. 100% power was definitely more effective, but the way it affected his personality was still too much for him to bear. He hated feeling like 'something' was dragging his attention to every minor environmental flaw. The speedup in calculation and design times weren't worth it unless he initiated 100% power in small bursts.

Despite this, Helen seemed to have no trouble coping.

_Maybe I wired something incorrectly? Perhaps it's active but not interfacing with her brain?_ Jason pondered.

Jason glanced down at the book in Helen's hand. It was closed, but she had her thumb wedged between the pages to hold her spot without a bookmark.

Jason's eyebrows jumped. "You're almost done reading the book?"

Helen nodded. "It's not a long one, silly boy. Less than 50,000 words."

"Yeah, but when did you start reading it?" Jason asked.

Helen thought for a moment. "I don't know... maybe five minutes ago? I actually read three other books before this one. I find that reading is so much easier now!"

Jason gasped. That confirmed it. Her MindCore was absolutely functioning properly. It was enhancing her reading speed to a superhuman level.

"You mind finishing the rest right now?" Jason asked.

This time, it was Helen who raised an eyebrow. "Well, I suppose so. It's a little awkward reading while a bunch of people are standing around watching me. Don't you think so?"

"Please." Jason said, pressing his palms together with a smile.

Helen shrugged. She was interrupted right at the end anyway, and she wanted to finish the book. Without overthinking it, she lifted the book back up, opened it, and her eyes started rapidly moving left and right, going down and up the pages.

Within three seconds, the flipped the page. Three seconds later, she flipped the next one. Then the next. After she got back into the 'groove' of reading, her speed increased faster and fast. She flipped the last few pages one after the other, barely an eyeblink of time from the moment the page revealed itself to the moment she started flipping to the next.

When she finished, Helen sighed. "Such a fascinating story about the fate of society if it keeps slipping into mass surveillance. I definitely recommend giving it a read."

"Do you even realize what you just did?" Jason asked. "Helen, you read forty-five pages in less than a minute."

"Oh? Did I?" Helen asked, her expression quizzical. "I guess time flies when you're enjoying yourself. That must have been terribly boring for you to sit there and watch me read, though."

Jason was amazed. For some reason, the negative effects of the MindCore were basically nonexistent when it came to Helen. She operated at 100% power without any issues, and didn't even notice anything was off.

"Helen, let's go inside and talk for a bit. I have some stuff I'd like to run past you." Jason said.

She and Harold looked at each other, then back at Jason. Harold shrugged. "Suit yerself, kid. I'm gonna make me a sammich."

He trotted away, leaving Jason and Sasha alone with Helen. They went into the study, where a collection of books sat inside shelves. At Catherine's recommendation, Jason had stocked the room just for Helen.

Helen crinkled her nose as she looked around. "I suppose I'll finish all of these pretty soon. Are there any other books you can get me?"

Jason nodded seriously. "I can do better than that. Helen, do you have any preferences when it comes to reading? Any favorite genres?"

"Not particularly." Helen said. "I do enjoy reading historical nonfiction, because it involves real people and events, but there's nothing wrong with westerns, sci-fi, fantasy, even manuals for cars will do the trick if there's nothing else."

Jason's eyes metaphorically flashed. He suddenly had a realization pop into his head. _Helen isn't as simple as I thought! She devours knowledge like no other. She'll read anything. This could be a chance!_

"Helen, if you want something to read, I actually took some time to create copies of every book on Earth. I made a massive library in an underground part of Aevum." Jason explained. "Even better, the books are all digitized. You can simply press your finger to interface with the data and download the books directly into your mind!"

Jason smiled expectantly at Helen, but her expression was one of disinterest. "I like reading books though. I like the way the books feel in my hands. If it's just some magical mumbo-jumbo, I don't think I'll enjoy them nearly as much..."

Jason blinked. He hadn't thought of this, but when Helen spoke, he immediately understood the type of person she was. She was quiet, introspective, and loved to let her imagination soar. The very act of turning a page was pure pleasure and dopamine to her.

A digitized library simply couldn't replace that.

"Hmm. Okay, in that case, I'll create a device that can instantly print out any of the books inside the Great Library of Aevum." Jason replied. "You can interface with a tool to decide what to read."

In an instant, Jason began rapidly building such a magical device on the spot. His mind whirred into action as a hundred simu-Jasons started rapidly iterating and building a prototype inside his MindCore. Within five minutes, he conjured an elastic and tasteful leather bracelet with a glowing gem on its top. Then he placed a tiny stool-shaped metal object with a flat metal surface on the table before Helen.

"Use the bracelet to connect to the library's database." Jason said. "Then you can pick any title to read, and it will materialize the book on top of this platform."

Helen's eyes shone excitedly. "Oh, how delightful! Let me try it out."

She put the bracelet on her left wrist, then touched it with her right hand. Her pupils momentarily blanked out as her consciousness dove into the library. For thirty seconds, she zoned out completely, losing awareness of her surroundings. The sight was a little eerie, causing Sasha to fidget a little.

"Is... is she okay?" Sasha asked Jason.

"She's fine. There are a _lot_ of books in there though. Give her a minute."

And so they did. Five minutes, actually. Helen returned to reality and blinked her eyes, a look of disbelief on her face.

"Amazing... I couldn't even see the tiniest portion of them. So many books... this will keep me busy for years to come!"

"I certainly hope so." Jason said with a smile. "I forcibly downloaded a portion of the Library into my head, but that amounted to only 5% of the collection. I didn't have time to properly read and digest them, so my gains weren't as big as I'd hoped. I have a feeling you'll accomplish way more than me once you put your mind to it."

Helen nodded. She touched the summoning platform, and within seconds a series of light particles started swirling around, transforming into a copy of _The Great Gatsby._ She picked it up and flipped through the first few pages before smiling and setting it down.

"Incredible. This Book Baker is really something! I'm getting excited, thinking of all the possibilities!"

"Book Baker?" Jason repeated. "That's what you're calling the materialization platform?"

"It feels right to me." She answered. "It's basically... cooking the books! And the name seems catchy."

Jason shrugged. If that was the name she wanted to use, he was fine with it. Who was he to criticize someone else's naming sense, after Hope, Hero City, and Echo?

"Nadia doesn't spend a lot of time reading books, and neither do I." Jason said. "If you can devour this library and really learn from it, you'll obtain the entire base of human knowledge! I was thinking of also collecting the Volgrim knowledge as well, so that'll be next on my priority list."

"That's very generous of you." Helen said, her face radiating joy. "I find it funny I'm going to be better at doing something I already enjoyed, but I just find books to be so enthralling!"

"Well, let's talk about other stuff your MindCore will allow you to do." Jason said, splitting his attention between Helen and Sasha equally. "The GenesisFrame is all about creativity. The more knowledge you possess, the better you'll be at designing objects, weapons, armor, and other such things. It offers an astounding boost to creative and engineering thought patterns. For Helen, I granted you a MindCore so you'd be able to assist Catherine in making artifacts for her to use in battle. For Sasha, your MindCore will allow you to weaponize your powers into more potent forms."

"But I'm only good at making clothes..." Sasha said hesitantly. "I appreciate the upgrade, 100%, but... that doesn't seem very useful for you."

"Clothing is only a step removed from armor-smithing, and your power allows you to rearrange the atoms of objects you touch. It's only a matter of time before you're able to start making armor that can save lives on the battlefield." Jason assured her. "And Helen, reading historical books is fine and dandy, don't let me rain on your parade or anything, but if you can focus on reading mechanical engineering textbooks and other such manuals from the hard sciences, you'll be able to quickly catch up on modern engineering paradigms. Futuristic ones, too, once I build a library of Volgrim knowledge."

"I can do that." Helen promised. "You've reunited me with my husband and given me access to millions of books. Shoring up my knowledge to help the war effort is the least I can do."

"There's a bit more than 'millions' of books in there, but I'm glad you agree with the general notion." Jason joked. "Now, let's talk about how to maximize the value of your MindCores. My MindCore is a v1, but yours are v4's. Yours should range from anywhere between two to three times the efficiency and capability of mine. That means if I were to do the same tasks as either of you, you'd finish in half the time, or less, compared to me. You should be utilizing your MindCores as often as possible so as to not waste them."

Jason started explaining some of the things he was doing to keep his MindCore active at all times. These were details he hadn't shared with anyone else, not even Nadia, though she probably already knew because of Echo. It wasn't that Jason was keeping his GenesisFrame's capabilities a secret, but more that people without one simply couldn't understand how it worked.

It would be like a mechanical engineer at a rocket ship company explaining to an average person on the street the latest specs on the rocket they'd just designed. The layperson simply wouldn't be capable of understanding anything they were hearing!

But since Sasha and Helen both had GenesisFrames, they were able to start immediately putting Jason's suggestions into use.

"So it's like that. Wow." Sasha said, as she started having her simu-Sashas get to work updating one of her old dress designs. "I can have five different designs working in parallel?"

"Ten, twenty, fifty, even." Jason said. "But that comes later. You should read a bunch of books on fashion design, metalworking, and so on. The more knowledgeable you are about how things work, the more optimized your MindCore's workloads will become."

He looked at Helen meaningfully. "And if you were to download books directly into your brain, you could read multiple of them at the same time. I know you prefer reading by flipping the pages, but it's a lot less optimal compared to the digitized method."

Helen seemed dissatisfied. "Hmm. Less optimal... you might be right. But it's just not the same thing if I'm not holding a book in my hands..."

"Oh, I know!" Sasha exclaimed. "Why not download the technical manuals and stuff, peruse those in your head, and just read the fun books out in the real world? That's like killing ten birds with one stone!"

Helen was enlightened. "That's a good idea... I think I might just do that."

Jason smiled. The two newest MindCore users were off to an excellent start.

Once he finished setting them up for success, he was looking forward to the next inductees into the Order of Big Brains...
